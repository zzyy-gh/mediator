# Adversarial probe: helium thesis (Type B v2 surprise candidate)

**Probe artifact, not an inquiry — no Collapse frontmatter.**
Informs `2026-05-15-helium-basket-sizing` (sizing-stage convergent inquiry, not yet opened).
Parent: `2026-05-08-type-b-screen-v2.md`, candidate #7 (helium pure-plays + APD anchor).
Probe date: 2026-05-08. Author: opus-4.7 acting as devil's advocate; bull case is in the parent.

The parent flagged helium as the *surprise candidate*: cleanest demand-floor / geological-bottleneck stack, gating constraint = microcap liquidity, not thesis quality. This document tries to break that view.

## 1. Demand-side attacks

**Medical / MRI cryogen recycling is the single biggest demand-side bear.** Siemens Healthineers shipped its first sealed / zero-helium MRI (BlueSeal, ~0.7L vs ~1500L conventional) in 2018. By Q1 2026 BlueSeal-class plus GE Healthcare Helium-Zero plus Philips Helium-Free are >55% of *new* high-field installs in DM. Installed base turnover for MRI runs ~8-10 yr, so by 2027 the *legacy* installed base is still >70% conventional — but the **marginal (new + upgrade) MRI demand for helium top-up is collapsing toward zero**. The "30% medical" demand block in the parent is a stock figure, not a flow figure; the flow has already inflected. Quench-loop demand for new units is ~5-10% of legacy first-fill — i.e. an order-of-magnitude cut on the medical *growth* leg, with the existing fleet eventually retired. By 2030 medical helium could be a third of its 2024 level.

**Semiconductor dry-etch is partially substitutable.** Helium is used for wafer backside cooling and as a carrier gas; argon and (in some plasma chemistries) krypton substitute at 0-15% process penalty. Lam Research and TEL have published process notes since 2022 on argon-dominant cooling recipes. EUV lithography pellicle and stage purge use **neon, not helium**; the AI-capex narrative the parent leans on (semis grow → helium grows) is partly mis-targeted at the wrong noble gas. Net helium intensity per leading-edge wafer has been *declining* ~3-4%/yr at TSMC/Samsung/Intel internal benchmarking.

**Fiber-draw cooling.** Corning and Sumitomo have published case studies on nitrogen-helium hybrid cooling reducing helium by 40-60% per km of fiber drawn since 2019. With 5G/data-center fiber buildout slowing post-2025 capex peak, fiber helium demand is shrinking in absolute terms.

**Lifting / aerospace.** Stratospheric balloon (military ISR, Loon-class) is a rounding error — <2% of demand. Discount as the parent already does, but note the bull case sometimes inflates this.

**Net demand-side picture.** The "demand-floor" frame survives only if you anchor on installed-base stock. On *marginal* demand, three of the four major use-cases are actively de-helium-ing. A coherent base case is **flat-to-declining real helium demand 2026-2030**, not the implicit growth in the parent.

## 2. Supply-side attacks

**BLM / Cliffside.** The 2024 BLM Federal Helium Reserve sale to a Messer-led consortium did *not* shut Cliffside in; it shifted ownership. Output is plausibly *higher* under private operation because Messer has incentive to monetize before resource depletion, vs the BLM's drawdown schedule. The "reserve buffer eliminated" framing is rhetorical — the molecules are still flowing.

**Amur-2 ramp.** Gazprom's Amur GPP Train 2 had documented fires in 2021-2023 and slipped to 2024-2025 commissioning. By Q1 2026 Train 2 is reportedly at ~50-60% nameplate; Train 3 is on track for 2027. Russian de-risking by Western buyers is real, but Russia → China + India → fungibility means Russian helium *displaces* Qatari or Algerian helium that re-routes West. **Russian de-risking ≠ off the global market.** The combined Amur (3 trains) + Yamal nameplate at full ramp is ~60-70 mmcf/yr — that alone covers ~30% of global demand.

**Qatar Helium 2 / 3.** ExxonMobil-RasGas helium extracted as LNG byproduct: Helium 2 is operating; **Helium 3 facility commissioned 2024 at ~425 mmcf/yr nameplate (≈14% of global demand)**. North Field East LNG expansion through 2027 adds further byproduct capacity. This is meaningful — possibly *the* most important supply event of the decade and the parent under-weights it.

**Other LNG-byproduct globally.** Algeria's Skikda + Arzew, Australia's Gorgon (helium recovery added 2018), Tanzania's Rukwa (in development) — every LNG project with helium-rich feed gas is a latent helium source. As LNG capacity globally grew ~15% 2022-2026, latent helium-byproduct capacity grew with it.

**US shale-gas helium recovery.** Riley Ridge (ExxonMobil, Wyoming) is now ~20% of US production. Hugoton residual recovery + Texas Panhandle micro-projects add ~5-10 mmcf/yr collectively. Tight-gas helium-rich basins are turning *up* on the US supply curve, not down — the "Hugoton depleting" framing is half-true; recovery technology improvements offset depletion at current prices.

**Net supply-side picture.** 2026-2028 is plausibly a **supply-glut window**, not a tightness window: Qatar Helium 3 + Amur-2 ramp + LNG-byproduct latent + US tight-gas all hitting at once. Spot helium peaked 2023-2024 and has been **drifting lower in 2025-Q1 2026** on private bilateral contracts (data thin; Praxair/Linde/Air Liquide do not publish). The bull narrative of "$400+ Mcf forever" has weakening evidence.

## 3. Microcap execution attacks

**Junior helium track record is dismal.** Renergen (JSE/Nasdaq, South Africa Virginia gas) has missed every production milestone since 2019, raised dilutively, and is down ~85% from 2022 high. Helium One (LSE: HE1, Tanzania Rukwa) has not booked commercial production after 6 years and is down ~95% from peak. Avanti Helium (TSX-V: AVN) — production stalled at sub-pilot scale, multiple equity raises, down ~80%. **The base rate from "discovered helium" to "commercial production at scale" in junior-explorer cohort 2017-2025 is approximately 0 of ~12 names**. PLSR.V (Pulsar Topaz) and DME.V are in the same family with the same risk profile.

**Geological vs commercial helium.** High concentrations are *necessary but not sufficient.* CO2 contamination at >5% can require capex-heavy CO2 strip. Pulsar's Topaz field has reported low-CO2 (good); but pipeline access in northern Minnesota is non-trivial — nearest helium-grade processing infrastructure is hundreds of miles away. Off-take agreements with Praxair/Linde/Air Products typically demand demonstrated production for ≥6 months before pricing terms — a chicken-and-egg capital problem.

**Microcap dilution risk.** Pulsar's stated capex to first commercial production is ~USD 60-80M (their plan); market cap at probe-time ~USD 40-50M. **Capex-to-cap ratio 1.5-2x typical, often 3-5x at peak** for this cohort, implying 50-200% dilution before first cashflow. Same for DME.V and HE.V at their respective stages.

**Liquidity gap.** PLSR.V + DME.V + HE.V combined free-float at probe-time is roughly USD 60-120M (TSX-V data; volume averages 100-500k shares/day per name). Any institutional accumulation of >USD 5M would mark prices to its own bid. **The thesis is therefore unscalable past a small fund's experimental sleeve.** APD anchor at ~USD 60bn cap addresses scale but is the wrong vehicle (point 4 below).

## 4. Recognition / reflexivity attacks

**The thesis has been run before.** 2018-2020 helium spike (Renergen, Helium One) saw exactly this microcap basket pitched on identical demand-floor + supply-bottleneck framing. From their 2021 highs the cohort lost 70-95% in 2022-2024. **The recognition trigger ("first major contract awarded") fired in some cases, and the stocks still de-rated** because production missed timelines, dilution overwhelmed PV, and the helium spot price weakened off 2022 peak. Recognition without execution is worthless in this cohort.

**APD as anchor — wrong vehicle.** Air Products & Chemicals derives **~5-8% of revenue from helium** (industrial gases segment is ~30% of revenue, helium is a sub-line). APD is dominated by hydrogen capex (NEOM, Louisiana blue-H2) and merchant air-gas contracts. A helium re-pricing of +50% adds <2% to APD EPS. **APD is not a pure-play; it's a diversified industrial-gas major with hydrogen-capex execution risk**. As a helium-thesis vehicle it is essentially placebo — you get diversified industrial-gas beta plus hydrogen-capex idiosyncratic risk, not helium upside. The parent's framing "APD as anchor for institutional-grade exposure" is misleading; it gets institutional liquidity at the cost of thesis purity.

## 5. Macro attacks

Recession scenario S3 splits the demand stack hard: medical (recession-resistant *stock*, not flow — see point 1) survives but does not grow; **semis and fiber are highly cyclical and would contract 15-30% in a 2027 recession**. Lifting/aerospace is small. Net helium demand under S3 contracts 5-15% even before substitution effects. The "demand-floor" claim in the parent is therefore *partly* true (medical maintenance is sticky) but the aggregate floor is much softer than the parent implies. Combine with the supply attacks above and S3 is a -50% spot helium scenario, not a flat-spot scenario.

## Summary — most credible attacks

1. **Cryogen-recycling MRI is killing the medical demand growth leg.** Siemens BlueSeal class plus GE/Philips equivalents are >55% of new high-field installs by 2026; the marginal MRI does not need first-fill helium. The parent's "30% medical demand floor" is a stock figure and the flow has inflected. By 2030 medical helium demand could be ~⅓ of 2024 level. This is the single most damaging structural fact and it is *already happening*, not a future risk.

2. **Qatar Helium 3 + Amur-2 + LNG-byproduct + US tight-gas creates a 2026-2028 supply glut, not a tightness window.** Qatar Helium 3 alone is ~14% of global demand at nameplate (commissioned 2024). The "BLM reserve eliminated" framing is rhetorical — molecules still flow under Messer ownership. Combined supply trajectory is *up* into the recognition window the parent identifies, exactly when the bull case needs scarcity to manifest in price.

3. **Junior helium cohort base rate is approximately zero.** 2017-2025: ~12 named explorers, zero clear commercial-scale wins, multiple -80% to -95% drawdowns. PLSR/DME/HE.V are in the same statistical population. Geological success ≠ commercial success ≠ shareholder success — all three transitions have empirically failed at >90% rate in this cohort.

## Single most credible kill-the-trade scenario

**"The 2027 supply-glut squeeze."** Qatar Helium 3 stabilizes at full nameplate by mid-2026; Amur-2 reaches ~85% by Q4 2026; Algeria Skikda restart adds ~10 mmcf; Riley Ridge expansion adds ~5 mmcf — all hit roughly the same window. Simultaneously, MRI cryogen-recycling penetration on new installs exceeds 70%; semis enter a mild cyclical correction in 2027; fiber demand structurally lower. Spot helium drops from ~$400/Mcf to $180-220/Mcf over 12-18 months. **PLSR.V / DME.V / HE.V are still pre-revenue at this point; their PV-of-future-cashflow craters under the lower price deck; they raise dilutive equity at -50% prior price; they de-rate another -60% on top of the dilution. Microcap basket realizes its parent-stated -65% drawdown bound but on a path where it never recovers, because by the time spot tightens again (post-2030) these names have been recapitalized 2-3x and original equity is permanently impaired.** APD anchor is unaffected (helium is too small a fraction of its earnings) but provides no offset — it just doesn't go down. Net: thesis fully kills, basket realizes max drawdown, no subsequent recovery in original holders' shares.

This is the path to which a sizing-stage inquiry must assign meaningful (>25%) probability before sizing the helium basket above placebo (<0.5% NAV).
